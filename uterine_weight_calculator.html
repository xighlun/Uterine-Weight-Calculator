How this works

Uterine volume is estimated with the prolate-ellipsoid formula, volume (cm³) = (π/6) × dimension1 × dimension2 × dimension3, the same approach used by Kung & Chang (1996). Weight is predicted from volume using a two-part (segmented) log-log regression fit to 17,809 hysterectomy cases from the Michigan Surgical Quality Collaborative (MSQC) registry, with the relationship allowed to change at a volume of 170 cm³. If fibroid status is specified, a refined version of the model (fit on 4,560 fibroid and 6,242 non-fibroid cases with known indication) is used instead. Predicted weight follows a right-skewed (log-normal) distribution rather than a symmetric bell curve, which is why the curve above is not centered on the estimate -- the shaded bands show the 80% and 95% prediction intervals, i.e. the range within which the actual specimen weight for an individual patient is expected to fall.

Research tool derived from a retrospective cohort of 17,809 hysterectomy cases in the Michigan Surgical Quality Collaborative (MSQC) registry. Provides a statistical estimate to support, not replace, clinical judgment. Not validated as a diagnostic or clinical decision-support device.
